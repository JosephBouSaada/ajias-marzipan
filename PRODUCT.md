## Users

Primary: people in the greater Boston area planning an occasion that needs a sweet table,
favors, or a gift — a wedding, a baptism or christening, a first communion, a birthday or
holiday party. They are usually planning weeks ahead, often on a phone, often comparing
against a bakery or a candy shop. Many are from communities where marzipan at a life
event is expected rather than novel (the archive shows Mediterranean/Middle Eastern
occasion conventions: sugared roses, swaddled-baby favors, chalice plaques).

Secondary: repeat gift buyers around holidays (Christmas, Easter, Valentine's) buying a
box rather than commissioning an event.

## Product Purpose

Ajia hand-makes marzipan — almond paste confection — in any shape and at any size, and
sells it across the greater Boston area. The site exists so a planner can (1) see that
the work is genuinely hand-sculpted and worth trusting with a once-in-a-life event, and
(2) start an order, either by picking from a listed catalog or by describing a custom
commission. Success is a submitted order inquiry.

## Positioning

Not a bakery and not a candy store: a single maker who sculpts marzipan to the occasion.
The same medium scales from one wrapped rose as a favor to a full centerpiece tray, and
the shapes are made to match the event — the baby for the baptism, the couple for the
wedding, the chalice for the communion. Range in one material, made by one pair of hands,
is the thing a neighboring business cannot truthfully copy.

## Operating Context

- Orders are commissioned ahead of a dated event; lead time and quantity matter more than
  browsing.
- Output is produced in trays, boxes, and individually cello-wrapped favors with ribbon.
- Serving formats seen in the archive: round gold centerpiece trays, long rectangular gold
  trays, gift boxes with gold cups (15 / 24 / 30-piece grids), glass jars, single wrapped
  pieces, lollipop-mounted figures.
- Occasions in the archive: weddings, baptisms/christenings, first communion, Christmas,
  Easter, Valentine's, birthdays and themed kids' parties.

## Capabilities and Constraints

  Sold singly (wrapped, ribboned), massed on trays, and as rose-topped hearts.

- **Flowers** — dahlias/chrysanthemums, poinsettias, daisies, fluted rosettes.
- **Chocolate-dipped marzipan** — dark and milk, sold by the jar.
- **Holiday boxes** — Christmas red/green assortments, Easter pastel eggs, bunnies, carrots.
- **Party / themed** — custom characters and colors to a party theme.

**Dietary (owner-stated, load-bearing):** the marzipan is **gluten-free, dairy-free, and
vegan**. This is a genuine differentiator at a mixed-guest sweet table, where most
alternatives (cake, cookies, cream pastry) exclude someone. It is a first-class product
fact, not a footnote — the owner has raised it twice unprompted.

**Scope of the claim, resolved with the owner:** the claim belongs to the marzipan, not to
the chocolate. Chocolate is the one component Ajia buys in rather than makes, so both
chocolate-dipped products sit outside the vegan and dairy-free claim:

- Milk-chocolate-dipped — contains dairy. Not dairy-free, not vegan. Stated plainly.
- Dark-chocolate-dipped — **not yet confirmed** by the owner. Many dark chocolates carry
  milk fat or are made on shared lines, so the site does not assert it. The card tells the
  visitor to ask her.

Neither chocolate product carries a dietary badge. Every other product does. Site copy says
"the marzipan is gluten-free, dairy-free and vegan" rather than an unqualified blanket
claim. An allergen and cross-contact statement is still owner-supplied and ships marked.

## Product Principles

1. **The photographs are the proof.** The work is visually convincing on its own; the site's
   job is to get out of its way and present it at scale, not to decorate around it.
2. **Occasion is the organizing idea, not product category.** People arrive with a wedding,
   not with a craving for almond paste.
3. **"Any shape and any size" must be demonstrated, not claimed.** The archive's range —
   one wrapped rose to a full tray — is the argument.
4. **Never invent commercial fact.** Prices, lead times, contact details and credentials are
   the owner's to supply; every gap ships visibly marked.
5. **One maker, by hand.** The voice is a person's, not a brand department's.
